# Certifying Measurement Incompatibility with a Photonic Quantum Switch

Determining whether two quantum measurements are compatible is a foundational question in quantum theory. In general, its resolution requires full process tomography followed by a semidefinite feasibility test, a procedure whose cost scales unfavourably with Hilbert-space dimension. The quantum SWITCH provides an operationally efficient alternative: by coherently applying two measurement processes in indefinite causal orders and measuring the control qubit, incompatibility is rendered directly accessible as a single interference signal, with sample complexity independent of system dimension.

We present a photonic implementation of this protocol for sharp (projective) polarization observables, for which the measured signal is exactly proportional to the mutual eigenspace disturbance, a rigorous incompatibility measure. In place of spatially separated interferometer arms, the switch control is encoded in a time-bin qubit, and polarization-dependent temporal walk-off induced by birefringent crystals implements dephasing in an arbitrary measurement basis. As temporal-shift operators commute, both causal orders yield identical output wavepackets without requiring active interferometric stabilization, yielding a protocol that is robust by construction. We describe the corresponding crystal design and measurement scheme, and outline the intended extension of the protocol from projective measurements (PVMs) to unsharp measurements (POVMs), for which commutativity is no longer necessary for joint measurability and the operational meaning of the switch signal must be established anew.
